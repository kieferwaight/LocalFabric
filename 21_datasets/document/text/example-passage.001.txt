The river bent twice before reaching the meadow, and on its far bank a
heron stood motionless in the reeds. Morning fog still clung to the
hollows, slow to disperse even after the sun had cleared the ridge. A
single fisherman, knee-deep in the shallows, cast and recast his line in
a rhythm so quiet that the heron, unbothered, did not look up.

Such mornings, the old guide claimed, were the only honest ones — when
the world withheld itself from being read too quickly, and a patient
observer could see the same shape twice before naming it.
